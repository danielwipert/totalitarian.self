See `claims.md`, "Evidence standard".
7. **The thesis sentence (§1, C1.1).** It says the Self "began as a necessary acknowledgment of human difference." After the §4 gates (2026-10-23), §4 says the Self grew out of a structure of personal *answerability*, which only later became an acknowledgment of difference. Does the thesis sentence change to match? **Resolved 2026-10-23:** yes; see C1.1 and `decisions.md`.
8. **When does answerability become difference?** §4 now needs a later moment where the Self starts to mean individual difference (candidates: Rousseau, the Romantics, Emerson). Which texts carry that step? **Answered 2026-10-23:** difference becomes a value from Rousseau to Emerson, always bounded by a shared frame; singularity is the mutation. See C4.15.
9. **When, and through whom, does difference lose its frame?** This is now §5's central event (C4.15). Candidates to test: Stirner, Whitman, Wilde, Nietzsche, the reception of Emerson (C6.9), 20th-century popular culture. **Partly answered 2026-10-23:** philosophy drops the frames in layers but never makes the Self its own origin; that is tested next in popular culture (C5.14).
10. **When does "self-expression" reach the consumer's own mouth (C8.6)?** After 1928 (Bernays). The likely sources are under copyright (Marchand, Frank, Riesman), so they need excerpts from copies in hand.
11. **When does the self stop borrowing its power (C5.14, step 2)?** Candidates: Wattles (1910, public domain), Hill (1937), postwar self-help and therapy (Rogers, Maslow; copyrighted).
