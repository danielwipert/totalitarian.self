> it is necessary to note, what has often been forgotten, that the Reformation meant not the elimination of the Church's control over everyday life, but rather the substitution of a new form of control for the previous one. It meant the repudiation of a control which was very lax, at that time scarcely perceptible in practice, and hardly more than formal, in favour of a regulation of the whole of conduct which, penetrating to all departments of private and public life, was infinitely burdensome and earnestly enforced. If the Self was a response to a freer economic situation (C4.5), Weber's answer is that the response was a new and stricter bound, not release from bounds. That fits C4.1.

> But this brings us to the world of judgments of value and of faith, with which this purely historical discussion need not be burdened.
