## Passages

### hobbes-leviathan.01 · I.xiii — "solitary, poore, nasty, brutish, and short"

- **Claims:** C4.31 (for)
- **Location:** ch. XIII, "The Incommodities Of Such A War"; `texts/hobbes-leviathan.txt` line 4033
- **Checked:** text file

> Whatsoever therefore is consequent to a time of Warre, where every man is Enemy to every man; the same is consequent to the time, wherein men live without other security, than what their own strength, and their own invention shall furnish them withall. In such condition, there is no place for Industry; [...] no Arts; no Letters; no Society; and which is worst of all, continuall feare, and danger of violent death; And the life of man, solitary, poore, nasty, brutish, and short.

Verified (spelling "poore"). The strongest text for C4.31: before the common power there is "no Society" at all; individuals come first and society is made. But the condition is pictured as the worst possible, not as a freedom to be valued.

### hobbes-leviathan.02 · I.xiii — justice belongs to "Society, not [...] Solitude"

- **Claims:** C4.31 (for)
- **Location:** ch. XIII, "In Such A Warre, Nothing Is Unjust"; line 4092
- **Checked:** text file

> To this warre of every man against every man, this also is consequent; that nothing can be Unjust. The notions of Right and Wrong, Justice and Injustice have there no place. Where there is no common Power, there is no Law: where no Law, no Injustice. [...] Justice, and Injustice are none of the Faculties neither of the Body, nor Mind. If they were, they might be in a man that were alone in the world, as well as his Senses, and Passions. They are Qualities, that relate to men in Society, not in Solitude.

The pre-social individual has passions but no justice. This is "for" C4.31 in its strong form, but it cuts against the idea that the separated Self keeps a morality of its own: for Hobbes moral standing comes *with* society.

### hobbes-leviathan.03 · I.xiii — Glory: valued "at the same rate he sets upon himselfe"

- **Claims:** C4.31 (complicates)
- **Location:** ch. XIII, "From Diffidence Warre"; line 3989
- **Checked:** text file

> Againe, men have no pleasure, (but on the contrary a great deale of griefe) in keeping company, where there is no power able to over-awe them all. For every man looketh that his companion should value him, at the same rate he sets upon himselfe: And upon all signes of contempt, or undervaluing, naturally endeavours [...] to extort a greater value from his contemners, by dommage; and from others, by the example.

Even in the state of nature the self is already turned toward others' eyes: a cause of war is the demand to be valued. The "solitary" individual is not self-sufficient; his self-rating needs confirmation (bears on §8, the self as performed for others).

### hobbes-leviathan.04 · I.x — Worth is "his Price"

- **Claims:** C4.31 (complicates), C4.5 (complicates)
- **Location:** ch. X, "Worth"; line 2782
- **Checked:** text file

> The Value, or WORTH of a man, is as of all other things, his Price; that is to say, so much as would be given for the use of his Power: and therefore is not absolute; but a thing dependant on the need and judgement of another. [...] And as in other things, so in men, not the seller, but the buyer determines the Price. For let a man (as most men do,) rate themselves as the highest Value they can; yet their true Value is no more than it is esteemed by others.

Market language applied to persons (useful to C4.5's economic story), but the conclusion is the reverse of self-ownership of worth: the buyer, not the self, sets it. The individual's value is social through and through.

### hobbes-leviathan.05 · I.xi — no "Summum Bonum"; "restlesse desire of Power after power"

- **Claims:** C4.31 (for)
- **Location:** ch. XI, "What Is Here Meant By Manners" and "A Restlesse Desire Of Power, In All Men"; lines 3074, 3095
- **Checked:** text file

> the Felicity of this life, consisteth not in the repose of a mind satisfied. For there is no such Finis Ultimus, (utmost ayme,) nor Summum Bonum, (greatest good,) as is spoken of in the Books of the old Morall Philosophers. [...] So that in the first place, I put for a generall inclination of all mankind, a perpetuall and restlesse desire of Power after power, that ceaseth onely in Death.

Hobbes denies any common end that would bind individuals into a natural community; each pursues his own desires without terminus. This supports C4.31's premise that the unit is the desiring individual, not a shared good.

### hobbes-leviathan.06 · I.xv — the laws of nature are "dictates of Reason" and, as God's word, "properly called Lawes"

- **Claims:** C4.31 (complicates)
- **Location:** ch. XV, closing paragraph; line 5248
- **Checked:** text file

> These dictates of Reason, men use to call by the name of Lawes; but improperly: for they are but Conclusions, or Theoremes concerning what conduceth to the conservation and defence of themselves; whereas Law, properly is the word of him, that by right hath command over others. But yet if we consider the same Theoremes, as delivered in the word of God, that by right commandeth all things; then are they properly called Lawes.

Verifies "dictates of Reason." Hobbes's state of nature is bounded only thinly: by self-interested theorems, which become binding law only as God's command. The bound is there, but it is a hedged one, much weaker than Locke's (locke-treatise.02).

### hobbes-leviathan.07 · I.xv — Compleasance: the unsociable man "cast out of Society"

- **Claims:** C4.31 (against)
- **Location:** ch. XV, "The Fifth, Mutuall accommodation, or Compleasance"; line 4924
- **Checked:** text file

> A fifth Law of Nature, is COMPLEASANCE; that is to say, "That every man strive to accommodate himselfe to the rest." For the understanding whereof, we may consider, that there is in mens aptnesse to Society; a diversity of Nature, rising from their diversity of Affections; not unlike to that we see in stones brought together for building of an Aedifice. [...] so also, a man that by asperity of Nature, will strive to retain those things which to himselfe are superfluous, and to others necessary; and for the stubbornness of his Passions, cannot be corrected, is to be left, or cast out of Society, as combersome thereunto. [...] The observers of this Law, may be called SOCIABLE, (the Latines call them Commodi;) The contrary, Stubborn, Insociable, Froward, Intractable.

Natural law itself commands the individual to fit himself to others like a stone to a building. Difference ("asperity") is the problem, not the value. The moral Self is ordered toward society, even if society is artificial.

### hobbes-leviathan.08 · I.xv — against Pride: "every man acknowledge other for his Equall"

- **Claims:** C4.21 (against), C4.31 (for)
- **Location:** ch. XV, "The Ninth, Against Pride"; line 4993
- **Checked:** text file

> The question who is the better man, has no place in the condition of meer Nature; where, (as has been shewn before,) all men are equall. The inequallity that now is, has been introduced by the Lawes civill. [...] as if Master and Servant were not introduced by consent of men, but by difference of Wit; which is not only against reason; but also against experience. [...] And therefore for the ninth Law of Nature, I put this, "That every man acknowledge other for his Equall by Nature." The breach of this Precept is Pride.

For C4.31: rank is made by consent, not given by nature, so the individual stands prior to status. Against C4.21's "valued for his own excellence": claiming to be "the better man" is the vice of Pride. Like the reformers, Hobbes founds on equality, not distinction.

### hobbes-leviathan.09 · I.xvi — "Person" as mask, actor, representer

- **Claims:** C4.10 (complicates)
- **Location:** ch. XVI, "A Person What" and "The Word Person, Whence"; lines 5264, 5282
- **Checked:** text file

> A PERSON, is he "whose words or actions are considered, either as his own, or as representing the words or actions of an other man, or of any other thing to whom they are attributed, whether Truly or by Fiction." [...] The word Person is latine: instead whereof the Greeks have Prosopon, which signifies the Face, as Persona in latine signifies the Disguise, or Outward Appearance of a man, counterfeited on the Stage; and somtimes more particularly that part of it, which disguiseth the face, as a Mask or Visard: And from the Stage, hath been translated to any Representer of speech and action, as well in Tribunalls, as Theaters. So that a Person, is the same that an Actor is, both on the Stage and in common Conversation; and to Personate, is to Act, or Represent himselfe, or an other [...]

Forty years before Locke, "person" is already a term of attribution ("to whom they are attributed"), but grounded in representation and outward appearance, not consciousness. So Locke's forensic person (C4.10) has a rival, theatrical-legal ancestry; and Hobbes's "Represent himselfe" is a striking early form of §8's self as performance.

### hobbes-leviathan.10 · I.xvi — the multitude made "One Person" by the representer

- **Claims:** C4.31 (for)
- **Location:** ch. XVI, "A Multitude Of Men, How One Person"; line 5421
- **Checked:** text file

> A Multitude of men, are made One Person, when they are by one man, or one Person, Represented; so that it be done with the consent of every one of that Multitude in particular. For it is the Unity of the Representer, not the Unity of the Represented, that maketh the Person One. [...] And because the Multitude naturally is not One, but Many [...]

The community has no unity of its own: "naturally" it is many, and it becomes one only through each individual's consent "in particular." This is C4.31 at its plainest.

### hobbes-leviathan.11 · II.xvii — bees and ants: men agree "by Covenant only, which is Artificiall"

- **Claims:** C4.31 (for)
- **Location:** ch. XVII, "Why Certain Creatures Without Reason, Or Speech, Do Neverthelesse Live In Society"; lines 5623, 5650
- **Checked:** text file

> Secondly, that amongst these creatures, the Common good differeth not from the Private; and being by nature enclined to their private, they procure thereby the common benefit. But man, whose Joy consisteth in comparing himselfe with other men, can relish nothing but what is eminent. [...] Lastly, the agreement of these creatures is Naturall; that of men, is by Covenant only, which is Artificiall [...]

Explicitly against Aristotle's political animal: human society is artificial. Yet the reason given is social: man's joy is "comparing himselfe with other men." The individual is prior to *society* but not independent of *others*.

### hobbes-leviathan.12 · II.xvii — "I Authorise and give up my Right of Governing my selfe"; the "Mortall God"

- **Claims:** C4.31 (complicates)
- **Location:** ch. XVII, "The Generation Of A Common-wealth"; line 5661
- **Checked:** text file

> This is more than Consent, or Concord; it is a reall Unitie of them all, in one and the same Person, made by Covenant of every man with every man, in such manner, as if every man should say to every man, "I Authorise and give up my Right of Governing my selfe, to this Man, or to this Assembly of men, on this condition, that thou give up thy Right to him, and Authorise all his Actions in like manner." This done, the Multitude so united in one Person, is called a COMMON-WEALTH, in latine CIVITAS. This is the Generation of that great LEVIATHAN, or rather (to speake more reverently) of that Mortall God, to which wee owe under the Immortall God, our peace and defence.

The individual's founding act is to give up self-government. Separation from community is the starting hypothesis, and its whole point is to end it in "a reall Unitie." And the Leviathan stands "under the Immortall God": the frame is still religious.

### hobbes-leviathan.13 · II.xxi — the subject's liberty includes "their own trade of life"

- **Claims:** C4.2 (for)
- **Location:** ch. XXI, "Liberty Of Subjects Consisteth In Liberty From Covenants"; line 6997
- **Checked:** text file

> The Liberty of a Subject, lyeth therefore only in those things, which in regulating their actions, the Soveraign hath praetermitted; such as is the Liberty to buy, and sell, and otherwise contract with one another; to choose their own aboad, their own diet, their own trade of life, and institute their children as they themselves think fit; & the like.

Even the absolutist assumes a private sphere of contract and chosen occupation, not a fixed station. Modest support for C4.2's economic freedom; note it is a liberty left over by the sovereign, not a right against him.

### hobbes-leviathan.14 · II.xxix — "the Law is the publique Conscience"

- **Claims:** C4.31 (against), C1.1 (complicates)
- **Location:** ch. XXIX, "Erroneous Conscience"; line 10668
- **Checked:** text file

> Another doctrine repugnant to Civill Society, is, that "Whatsoever a man does against his Conscience, is Sinne;" and it dependeth on the presumption of making himself judge of Good and Evill. For a mans Conscience, and his Judgement is the same thing; and as the Judgement, so also the Conscience may be erroneous. Therefore, though he that is subject to no Civill Law, sinneth in all he does against his Conscience, because he has no other rule to follow but his own reason; yet it is not so with him that lives in a Common-wealth; because the Law is the publique Conscience, by which he hath already undertaken to be guided. Otherwise in such diversity, as there is of private Consciences, which are but private opinions, the Common-wealth must needs be distracted [...]

Hobbes names the Reformation doctrine of private conscience (C1.1's answerability) as seditious. The individual is prior only to be absorbed: once society exists, conscience becomes public. Hobbes's starting point is individualist; where he ends up is the opposite of a Self that has separated from community.

### hobbes-leviathan.15 · III.xl — "inward Thought, and beleef [...] fall not under obligation"

- **Claims:** C4.30 (complicates), C4.31 (complicates)
- **Location:** ch. XL, under "Abraham Had The Sole Power Of Ordering The Religion Of His Own People"; line 15226
- **Checked:** text file

> And Consequently in every Common-wealth, they who have no supernaturall Revelation to the contrary, ought to obey the laws of their own Soveraign, in the externall acts and profession of Religion. As for the inward Thought, and beleef of men, which humane Governours can take no notice of, (for God onely knoweth the heart) they are not voluntary, nor the effect of the laws, but of the unrevealed will, and of the power of God; and consequently fall not under obligation.

This is where Hobbes does separate something: inward belief, from the sovereign's reach. But the inner self is left to God, not to itself, and all outward religious life stays public. The split is inner/outer, not self/community.
