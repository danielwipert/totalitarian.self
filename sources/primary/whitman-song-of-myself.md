# Whitman, "Song of Myself" (*Leaves of Grass*, 1855; text of the 1891–92 edition)

- **Edition used:** original English
- **Text file:** `texts/whitman-leaves-of-grass.txt` (clean), Book III ("Song of Myself" runs lines 979–3047)
- **Standard edition** (for final citations): *Leaves of Grass*, Norton Critical Edition
- **Citation form:** section number, plus this file's line
- **Edition caution:** the poem was revised across editions from 1855 to 1891–92; the 1855 text is untitled and has no section numbers. Everything below is the 1891–92 text. Lines that carry an argument (e.g. §1's lineage lines, §24's self-naming) should be checked against the 1855 text before the essay dates them to 1855 (see To do).
- **Claims to test:** C5.9, C5.11, C6.*, C5.3, C5.6, C4.19, C4.1 (body as limit)

## Reading summary

Whitman's Self does both things the key question separates, often in the same line, so "contain *or* share" does not divide cleanly. There are two movements. **Sharing** (the frame kept): the opening "every atom belonging to me as good belongs to you" (.01), "not original with me, / If they are not yours as much as mine they are nothing" (.06), "I will accept nothing which all cannot have their counterpart of on the same terms" (.08), and "You must travel it for yourself" (.15). Here the Self is a sample of what everyone equally is, as Montaigne's was, and a channel ("Through me"), as Emerson's was. **Containing** (the frame absorbed): "All this I swallow [...] it becomes mine" and "I do not ask the wounded person how he feels, I myself become the wounded person" (.10), and "I do not ask who you are [...] You can do nothing and be nothing but what I will infold you" (.12). Here the other's own voice is replaced by the poet's. Whitman catches himself once ("on the verge of a usual mistake", §38) and goes back to being "one of an average unending procession".

What is new compared with Emerson: the frame is not an impersonal Over-Soul but *the democratic mass and the body*. God is kept but made a sibling ("the spirit of God is the brother of my own", .04) and then outranked ("nothing, not God, is greater to one than one's self is", .17). The gods are absorbed into the Self and handed out to everyone (.13). And the Self is explicitly **not** prior to its world: it is woven "of these" (.05), it is the *product* of cosmic preparation (.14), and it cannot say "whence the cause of my faintest wish" (.09). It is also openly unanswerable ("I do not trouble my spirit to vindicate itself or be understood", .07), and that is the first clean break with the answerability frame in the §4b line: no last trumpet, no jury.

Verdict for the essay: Whitman is the Over-Soul made democratic *and* bodily, with an imperial appetite that can tip into containment. He does not meet SPEC §2's "singularity" (self-originating, causally prior, cut from relation). He does drop answerability and outrank God, which is a different loss from the one C5.9 names.

## Passages

### whitman-song.01 · "Song of Myself" §1 — "every atom belonging to me as good belongs to you"; "Creeds and schools in abeyance"

- **Claims:** C5.11 (complicates), C6.2 (complicates), C5.3 (complicates)
- **Location:** §1; `texts/whitman-leaves-of-grass.txt` lines 982–984, 989–998
- **Checked:** text file

> I celebrate myself, and sing myself, / And what I assume you shall assume, / For every atom belonging to me as good belongs to you. [...]
>
> My tongue, every atom of my blood, form’d from this soil, this air, / Born here of parents born here from parents the same, and their / parents the same, [...]
>
> Creeds and schools in abeyance, / Retiring back a while sufficed at what they are, but never forgotten, / I harbor for good or bad, I permit to speak at every hazard, / Nature without check with original energy.

C5.11's own proof-text is a *sharing* line, not a containing one: the atom belongs to you "as good" as to me, so it is common property, not the Self's enlargement. But "what I assume you shall assume" sets the terms from the Self's side. The Self is made of soil and lineage (an inherited order, not none: C6.2), and creeds are suspended "a while [...] but never forgotten", not abolished.

### whitman-song.02 · §2 — "You shall not look through my eyes either [...] filter them from your self"

- **Claims:** C5.3 (complicates), C4.19 (for), C6.2 (for)
- **Location:** §2; lines 1032–1039
- **Checked:** text file

> Stop this day and night with me and you shall possess the origin of / all poems, [...] / You shall no longer take things at second or third hand, nor look through / the eyes of the dead, nor feed on the spectres in books, / You shall not look through my eyes either, nor take things from me, / You shall listen to all sides and filter them from your self.

Emerson's refusal of inherited authority (cf. emerson-selfreliance.06), extended even to the poet's own authority. Note the image: the self is a *filter* of "all sides", which is SPEC §2's healthy lens, not a source. Against that, "possess the origin of all poems" promises the reader an origin.

### whitman-song.03 · §4 — "they are not the Me myself"; "Both in and out of the game"

- **Claims:** C5.6 (for), C5.9 (complicates), C6.* (complicates)
- **Location:** §4; lines 1088–1105
- **Checked:** text file

> Trippers and askers surround me, / People I meet, the effect upon me of my early life or the ward and / city I live in, or the nation, [...] / Battles, the horrors of fratricidal war, the fever of doubtful news, / the fitful events; / These come to me days and nights and go from me again, / But they are not the Me myself. / Apart from the pulling and hauling stands what I am, / Stands amused, complacent, compassionating, idle, unitary, [...] / Both in and out of the game and watching and wondering at it.

The clearest case in the poem of a Self held *apart from* its history, upbringing, city, nation and war. This is C5.6's "prior to experience" in the form of a witness behind experience. But it is "compassionating" and "in [...] the game" as well as out, so it is not cut from relation.

### whitman-song.04 · §5 — "the spirit of God is the brother of my own"; "a kelson of the creation is love"

- **Claims:** C4.19 (complicates), C5.11 (complicates), C5.9 (against)
- **Location:** §5; lines 1126–1132
- **Checked:** text file

> Swiftly arose and spread around me the peace and knowledge that pass / all the argument of the earth, / And I know that the hand of God is the promise of my own, / And I know that the spirit of God is the brother of my own, / And that all the men ever born are also my brothers, and the women / my sisters and lovers, / And that a kelson of the creation is love,

The founding vision of the poem keeps a frame, and the frame is relational: God beside the Self as "brother" (not inside it, as in emerson-oversoul.06), all people as siblings, and love as the keel of creation. This is a frame of kinship, not of an impersonal Over-Soul.

### whitman-song.05 · §15–16 — "of these one and all I weave the song of myself"; "Of every hue and caste am I"

- **Claims:** C5.11 (for, and complicates), C6.* (for: the national catalogue), C5.6 (against), C5.3 (against)
- **Location:** §15 end, §16; lines 1497–1499, 1507–1508, 1527–1537
- **Checked:** text file

> And these tend inward to me, and I tend outward to them, / And such as it is to be of these more or less I am, / And of these one and all I weave the song of myself.
>
> I am of old and young, [...] / One of the Nation of many nations, the smallest the same and the / largest the same, [...] / Of every hue and caste am I, of every rank and religion, [...] / I resist any thing better than my own diversity, / Breathe the air but leave plenty after me, / And am not stuck up, and am in my place. / (The moth and the fish-eggs are in their place, / The bright suns I see and the dark suns I cannot see are in their place, / The palpable is in its place and the impalpable is in its place.)

This is the containing Self C5.11 predicts ("Of every hue and caste am I"), and its diversity is American (the "Nation of many nations"). But the Self is woven *of* others, not prior to them, and the traffic goes both ways ("tend inward [...] tend outward"). It ends "in my place" inside an order where everything has its place, which is a frame kept, not dropped.

### whitman-song.06 · §17 — "they are not original with me"

- **Claims:** C5.3 (against), C5.9 (against), C5.11 (against "contain"; for "share")
- **Location:** §17; lines 1540–1547
- **Checked:** text file

> These are really the thoughts of all men in all ages and lands, they / are not original with me, / If they are not yours as much as mine they are nothing, or next to nothing, [...] / This is the grass that grows wherever the land is and the water is, / This the common air that bathes the globe.

The strongest single "against" passage. The Self disclaims originality outright and makes common possession the test of worth. It is close to Montaigne's self as a sample of the common form (montaigne-essays.03).

### whitman-song.07 · §20 — "I do not trouble my spirit to vindicate itself or be understood"; "I exist as I am, that is enough"

- **Claims:** C5.9 (for: answerable to nothing), C4.15 (for: the first clean break with the answerability frame), C5.11 (complicates)
- **Location:** §20; lines 1609, 1615–1616, 1627–1637
- **Checked:** text file

> Why should I pray? why should I venerate and be ceremonious? [...]
>
> In all people I see myself, none more and not one a barley-corn less, / And the good or bad I say of myself I say of them. [...]
>
> I know I am august, / I do not trouble my spirit to vindicate itself or be understood, / I see that the elementary laws never apologize, [...] / I exist as I am, that is enough, / If no other in the world be aware I sit content, / And if each and all be aware I sit content. / One world is aware and by far the largest to me, and that is myself,

Compare Rousseau, whose uniqueness came straight paired with the last trumpet and the reader as jury (C4.16, C4.17). Here there is no court: no prayer, no vindication, no need to be understood, and the Self is its own largest audience. That fits C5.9's "answerable to nothing beyond itself". But it sits beside strict parity ("none more and not one a barley-corn less").

### whitman-song.08 · §24 — "a kosmos"; "the sign of democracy"; "Through me"

- **Claims:** C5.11 (complicates), C4.19 (for: the Self as channel), C5.3 (against), C6.1 (for, as ideology), C5.9 (against)
- **Location:** §24; lines 1761–1763, 1769–1777, 1779–1780
- **Checked:** text file

> Walt Whitman, a kosmos, of Manhattan the son, / Turbulent, fleshy, sensual, eating, drinking and breeding, / No sentimentalist, no stander above men and women or apart from them, [...]
>
> Whoever degrades another degrades me, / And whatever is done or said returns at last to me. / Through me the afflatus surging and surging, through me the current / and index. / I speak the pass-word primeval, I give the sign of democracy, / By God! I will accept nothing which all cannot have their / counterpart of on the same terms. / Through me many long dumb voices, / Voices of the interminable generations of prisoners and slaves,

This is where "difference and democracy merge", as C5.11 says. But the merge is a *rule of equal terms*, not containment: "nothing which all cannot have their counterpart of". The Self is a conduit ("Through me"), Emerson's channel (C4.19), not a source. "[N]o stander [...] apart from them" is an explicit refusal of difference cut from relation.

### whitman-song.09 · §24 — "Divine am I inside and out"; "the spread of my own body"; "nor whence the cause of my faintest wish"

- **Claims:** C4.1 (complicates: the body as limit), C4.19 (complicates), C5.3 (against), C5.9 (for: creeds outranked)
- **Location:** §24; lines 1797–1808, 1828–1832
- **Checked:** text file

> I believe in the flesh and the appetites, / Seeing, hearing, feeling, are miracles, and each part and tag of me / is a miracle. / Divine am I inside and out, and I make holy whatever I touch or am / touch’d from, / The scent of these arm-pits aroma finer than prayer, / This head more than churches, bibles, and all the creeds. / If I worship one thing more than another it shall be the spread of / my own body, or any part of it, [...]
>
> I dote on myself, there is that lot of me and all so luscious, / Each moment and whatever happens thrills me with joy, / I cannot tell how my ankles bend, nor whence the cause of my faintest wish, / Nor the cause of the friendship I emit, nor the cause of the / friendship I take again.

The §4 pattern says the body was a limit that the later Self dropped (Descartes's union, C4.7). Whitman does the opposite: he does not drop the body, he deifies it. Emerson's God-inside becomes God-in-the-flesh, and the body outranks "churches, bibles, and all the creeds". But the doting Self cannot name the cause of its own wishes or friendships, which is a plain denial that it is their source (C5.3).

### whitman-song.10 · §33, §38 — "All this I swallow [...] it becomes mine"; "on the verge of a usual mistake"

- **Claims:** C5.11 (for, then complicates), C5.9 (complicates)
- **Location:** §33, lines 2247–2248, 2267–2270; §38, lines 2448–2451, 2464–2465
- **Checked:** text file

> All this I swallow, it tastes good, I like it well, it becomes mine, / I am the man, I suffer’d, I was there. [...]
>
> Agonies are one of my changes of garments, / I do not ask the wounded person how he feels, I myself become the / wounded person, / My hurts turn livid upon me as I lean on a cane and observe.

> Enough! enough! enough! / Somehow I have been stunn’d. Stand back! / Give me a little time beyond my cuff’d head, slumbers, dreams, gaping, / I discover myself on the verge of a usual mistake. [...]
>
> I troop forth replenish’d with supreme power, one of an average / unending procession,

The strongest "contain" evidence: suffering is "swallow[ed]" and "becomes mine", and the wounded person is not asked; the poet becomes him. The other's own voice is replaced, which is the frame absorbed into the Self. Then the poem itself pulls back (§38). After a run of identifications that ends at the crucifixion, Whitman calls it "a usual mistake" and returns as "one of an average unending procession".

### whitman-song.11 · §40 — "I do not ask who you are [...] what I will infold you"

- **Claims:** C5.11 (for), C5.9 (for), C5.3 (for)
- **Location:** §40; lines 2502–2503, 2511–2512, 2533–2535
- **Checked:** text file

> Behold, I do not give lectures or a little charity, / When I give I give myself. [...]
>
> I do not ask who you are, that is not important to me, / You can do nothing and be nothing but what I will infold you. [...]
>
> Sleep--I and they keep guard all night, / Not doubt, not decease shall dare to lay finger upon you, / I have embraced you, and henceforth possess you to myself,

The plainest containment in the poem: the other's identity is "not important", and the other can "be nothing but what I will infold you". Here difference is not cut from relation. Relation becomes possession, and the other's difference is the thing dropped. This is the passage C5.11 should cite, not §1.

### whitman-song.12 · §41 — "Taking myself the exact dimensions of Jehovah"; "becoming already a creator"

- **Claims:** C4.19 (for: comparison, gods absorbed into the Self), C5.3 (for), C5.6 (complicates), C5.11 (complicates)
- **Location:** §41; lines 2546–2548, 2554–2562, 2581–2586
- **Checked:** text file

> Magnifying and applying come I, / Outbidding at the start the old cautious hucksters, / Taking myself the exact dimensions of Jehovah, [...] / Taking them all for what they are worth and not a cent more, / Admitting they were alive and did the work of their days, / (They bore mites as for unfledg’d birds who have now to rise and fly / and sing for themselves,) / Accepting the rough deific sketches to fill out better in myself, / bestowing them freely on each man and woman I see, / Discovering as much or more in a framer framing a house, [...]
>
> The supernatural of no account, myself waiting my time to be one of / the supremes, / The day getting ready for me when I shall do as much good as the / best, and be as prodigious; / By my life-lumps! becoming already a creator,

This goes past Emerson. The gods are not channelled; they are priced ("not a cent more"), absorbed ("to fill out better in myself") and then redistributed equally to "each man and woman". The Self becomes "a creator", the nearest the poem comes to C5.3. But it is a creator "waiting my time" and on a par with the carpenter, which is a democracy of creators, not one singular one.

### whitman-song.13 · §42 — "This is the city and I am one of the citizens"; "I know perfectly well my own egotism"

- **Claims:** C6.5 (complicates), C6.2 (complicates), C5.11 (for)
- **Location:** §42; lines 2616–2627, 2636–2638
- **Checked:** text file

> Here and there with dimes on the eyes walking, [...] / Tickets buying, taking, selling, but in to the feast never once going, / Many sweating, ploughing, thrashing, and then the chaff for payment / receiving, / A few idly owning, and they the wheat continually claiming. / This is the city and I am one of the citizens, / Whatever interests the rest interests me, politics, wars, markets, / newspapers, schools, / The mayor and councils, banks, tariffs, steamships, factories, / stocks, stores, real estate and personal estate. [...]
>
> I know perfectly well my own egotism, / Know my omnivorous lines and must not write any less, / And would fetch you whoever you are flush with myself.

America's commercial city is present, with its institutions (mayor, councils, banks, schools). That is evidence against "no inherited order" read as "no institutions". It is also criticized ("A few idly owning"), so capital is not celebrated here (C6.5). The poet admits the containing appetite as "egotism" and "omnivorous", and aims it at bringing "you" level ("flush") with himself.

### whitman-song.14 · §44 — "Immense have been the preparations for me"

- **Claims:** C5.6 (against), C5.3 (against), C5.9 (complicates)
- **Location:** §44; lines 2731–2732, 2752–2769
- **Checked:** text file

> I do not call one greater and one smaller, / That which fills its period and place is equal to any. [...]
>
> Immense have been the preparations for me, / Faithful and friendly the arms that have help’d me. / Cycles ferried my cradle, rowing and rowing like cheerful boatmen, [...] / Before I was born out of my mother generations guided me, [...] / For it the nebula cohered to an orb, / The long slow strata piled to rest it on, [...] / All forces have been steadily employ’d to complete and delight me, / Now on this spot I stand with my robust soul.

A direct test of SPEC §2 ("causally prior to one's world"). Here the Self is causally *posterior*: the product of the nebula, strata and "generations", helped by "faithful and friendly" arms. The cosmos converges *on* the Self, a grand egotism, but as its outcome, not its cause. Equality is restated just before ("equal to any").

### whitman-song.15 · §46 — "I have no chair, no church, no philosophy"; "You must travel it for yourself"; "you shall repay the same service to me"

- **Claims:** C6.2 (for, as ideology), C6.3 (for: the open road), C4.13 (for: answerability kept), C5.9 (against)
- **Location:** §46; lines 2825–2835, 2844–2847, 2855–2856
- **Checked:** text file

> I tramp a perpetual journey, (come listen all!) / My signs are a rain-proof coat, good shoes, and a staff cut from the woods, / No friend of mine takes his ease in my chair, / I have no chair, no church, no philosophy, / I lead no man to a dinner-table, library, exchange, / But each man and each woman of you I lead upon a knoll, / My left hand hooking you round the waist, / My right hand pointing to landscapes of continents and the public road. / Not I, not any one else can travel that road for you, / You must travel it for yourself. [...]
>
> If you tire, give me both burdens, and rest the chuff of your hand / on my hip, / And in due time you shall repay the same service to me, / For after we start we never lie by again. [...]
>
> I answer that I cannot answer, you must find out for yourself.

The American host conditions as Whitman sings them: no chair, church, school or exchange, only "continents and the public road" (C6.2, C6.3). These are claims about ideals, not proof of conditions. "You must travel it for yourself" is the §4 answerability in its oldest form: no one can stand in for you (cf. Luther, C4.3). It comes with an arm round the waist and mutual service, so the solitude is not asocial.

### whitman-song.16 · §47 — "learns under it to destroy the teacher"; "I act as the tongue of you"

- **Claims:** C5.9 (complicates), C4.19 (for), C5.11 (complicates)
- **Location:** §47; lines 2874–2879, 2889, 2895–2896
- **Checked:** text file

> I am the teacher of athletes, / He that by me spreads a wider breast than my own proves the width of my own, / He most honors my style who learns under it to destroy the teacher. / The boy I love, the same becomes a man not through derived power, / but in his own right, [...]
>
> I teach straying from me, yet who can stray from me? [...]
>
> (It is you talking just as much as myself, I act as the tongue of you, / Tied in your mouth, in mine it begins to be loosen’d.)

Self-grounding taught to others, "not through derived power, but in his own right", near C5.9's "its own ground". But the poem undercuts it at once: "who can stray from me?" (containment), and the poet's voice is "the tongue of you" (shared). The teaching to be independent is itself received from a teacher.

### whitman-song.17 · §48 — "nothing, not God, is greater to one than one's self is"; "without sympathy walks to his own funeral"

- **Claims:** C5.9 (for, and complicates), C4.19 (complicates), C4.1 (for: the God limit dropped)
- **Location:** §48; lines 2926–2930, 2940–2950
- **Checked:** text file

> I have said that the soul is not more than the body, / And I have said that the body is not more than the soul, / And nothing, not God, is greater to one than one’s self is, / And whoever walks a furlong without sympathy walks to his own / funeral drest in his shroud, [...]
>
> And I say to mankind, Be not curious about God, / For I who am curious about each am not curious about God, [...] / I hear and behold God in every object, yet understand God not in the least, / Nor do I understand who there can be more wonderful than myself. [...] / In the faces of men and women I see God, and in my own face in the glass,

The decisive line for the God limit: the Self outranks God ("nothing, not God, is greater"). That is further than Emerson, who has the Self *become* God by worship (emerson-oversoul.06). It goes with the instruction to turn curiosity from God to "each". But in the same breath the Self without "sympathy" is dead. God is seen in the faces of others before one's own, and soul and body are kept level.

### whitman-song.18 · §51 — "I am large, I contain multitudes"

- **Claims:** C5.11 (complicates), C4.19 (for)
- **Location:** §51; lines 3006–3007, 3013–3020
- **Checked:** text file (verified: the 1891–92 text puts the line in parentheses)

> The past and present wilt--I have fill’d them, emptied them. / And proceed to fill my next fold of the future. [...]
>
> Do I contradict myself? / Very well then I contradict myself, / (I am large, I contain multitudes.) / I concentrate toward them that are nigh, I wait on the door-slab. / Who has done his day’s work? who will soonest be through with his supper? / Who wishes to walk with me?

Verified, but in context the "multitudes" answer a charge of self-*contradiction*. They are inner plurality, not other people absorbed. It is Emerson's "foolish consistency" (emerson-selfreliance.04) made into a boast. The next lines turn outward to "them that are nigh" with an invitation ("Who wishes to walk with me?"), not an enclosure.

### whitman-song.19 · §52 — "I too am untranslatable"; "look for me under your boot-soles"

- **Claims:** C4.1 (complicates: the body as limit kept), C5.9 (complicates), C5.7 (against, for Whitman: mortality acknowledged)
- **Location:** §52; lines 3025–3029, 3035–3047
- **Checked:** text file

> The spotted hawk swoops by and accuses me, he complains of my gab / and my loitering. / I too am not a bit tamed, I too am untranslatable, / I sound my barbaric yawp over the roofs of the world. [...]
>
> I depart as air, I shake my white locks at the runaway sun, / I effuse my flesh in eddies, and drift it in lacy jags. / I bequeath myself to the dirt to grow from the grass I love, / If you want me again look for me under your boot-soles. [...] / Failing to fetch me at first keep encouraged, / Missing me one place search another, / I stop somewhere waiting for you.

"Untranslatable" is the poem's nearest word to singularity, but it is shared: "I *too*", like the hawk. The end accepts the body's limit, death and decomposition, as the Self's fate and turns it into common nourishment. The last word is relation: "waiting for you". Against C5.7's charge that the totalized Self represses mortality; this one sings it.

### whitman-inscriptions.01 · "One's-Self I Sing" (Inscriptions) — "a simple separate person, / Yet utter the word Democratic, the word En-Masse"

- **Claims:** C5.11 (complicates), C6.1 (for, as ideology), C4.1 (complicates: the body)
- **Location:** Book I, "One's-Self I Sing"; lines 58–64. Cf. "Song of Myself" §23, line 1730 ("And mine a word of the modern, the word En-Masse.")
- **Checked:** text file

> One’s-self I sing, a simple separate person, / Yet utter the word Democratic, the word En-Masse. / Of physiology from top to toe I sing, / Not physiognomy alone nor brain alone is worthy for the Muse, I say / the Form complete is worthier far, / The Female equally with the Male I sing.

Whitman's own later gloss on the program (the 1891–92 lead poem). The separate person and the mass are held together by "Yet": neither absorbs the other. "[N]or brain alone" is an explicit refusal of the Self-as-Mind (C5.8, C4.7): the whole body is the subject.
