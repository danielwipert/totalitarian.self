## Passages

### stirner-ego.01 · Preface, "All Things are Nothing to Me" — every cause but mine

- **Claims:** C5.10 (for), C5.9 (for)
- **Location:** preface; `texts/stirner-ego-and-its-own.txt` lines 584–589, 664–670
- **Checked:** text file

> What is not supposed, to be my concern! First and foremost, the Good Cause, then God's cause, the cause of mankind, of truth, of freedom, of humanity, of justice; further, the cause of my people, my prince, my fatherland; finally, even the cause of Mind, and a thousand other causes. Only _my_ cause is never to be my concern. [...]
>
> The divine is God's concern; the human, man's. My concern is neither the divine nor the human, not the true, good, just, free, etc., but solely what is _mine_, and it is not a general one, but is--_unique_, as I am unique.
>
> Nothing is more to me than myself!

(Footnote markers [2], [3], [5] omitted; [5] glosses "unique" as *einzig*.) The frames are listed and dropped by name: God, mankind, humanity, people, fatherland, Mind. This is the plainest support for C5.10's list. Note that the uniqueness is non-comparative ("not a general one"), not a difference from others.

### stirner-ego.02 · Preface — "the creative nothing"

- **Claims:** C5.3 (for), C5.6 (for), C5.10 (complicates)
- **Location:** preface; lines 649–659
- **Checked:** text file

> God and mankind have concerned themselves for nothing, for nothing but themselves. Let me then likewise concern myself for _myself_, who am equally with God the nothing of all others, who am my all, who am the only one.
>
> If God, if mankind, as you affirm, have substance enough in themselves to be all in all to themselves, then I feel that _I_ shall still less lack that, and that I shall have no complaint to make of my "emptiness." I am nothing in the sense of emptiness, but I am the creative nothing, the nothing out of which I myself as creator create everything.

(Marker [4], "the only one" = *der Einzige*, omitted.) This is the self as source in the strongest terms in the book (C5.3, C5.6). But the self takes over God's *structure* ("equally with God", "all in all") rather than standing with no frame; and its ground is a "nothing", not a substance (see .17).

### stirner-ego.03 · Part First, II.ii §1 "The Spirit" — God moved inside is still God

- **Claims:** C4.19 (for), C5.10 (for)
- **Location:** Part First, "The Moderns", §1 "The Spirit"; lines 1580–1593
- **Checked:** text file

> To this we reply: The supreme being is indeed the essence of man, but, just because it is his _essence_ and not he himself, it remains quite immaterial whether we see it outside him and view it as "God," or find it in him and call it "Essence of Man" or "Man." _I_ am neither God nor _Man_, neither the supreme essence nor my essence, and therefore it is all one in the main whether I think of the essence as in me or outside me. Nay, we really do always think of the supreme being as in both kinds of otherworldliness, the inward and outward, at once; for the "Spirit of God" is, according to the Christian view, also "our spirit," and "dwells in us." It dwells in heaven and dwells in us; we poor things are just its "dwelling," [...]

(Markers [17], [18] omitted.) Aimed at Feuerbach, but it is an exact description of the Emersonian move in C4.19: God moved inside the Self is still a frame, and the Self is "just its dwelling" (Emerson's "channel"). Stirner counts the inward move as keeping the frame, which supports the bridge finding that difference before him stayed framed.

### stirner-ego.04 · Part First, II.ii §3 "The Hierarchy" — conscience as inner police

- **Claims:** C4.17 (for), C5.10 (for)
- **Location:** Part First, "The Moderns", §3 "The Hierarchy"; lines 3486–3497
- **Checked:** text file

> Protestantism has actually put a man in the position of a country governed by secret police. The spy and eavesdropper, "conscience," watches over every motion of the mind, and all thought and action is for it a "matter of conscience," _i. e._ police business. This tearing apart of man into "natural impulse" and "conscience" (inner populace and inner police) is what constitutes the Protestant. [...] If one does not free himself from conscience, the consciousness of the sacred, he may act unconscientiously indeed, but never consciencelessly.

The inward court of C4.17 (conscience as the source of answerability) is named and rejected. Stirner treats the inward move as a deepening of servitude, not a liberation: to be free of the frame one must be free of conscience itself.

### stirner-ego.05 · Part First, III §3 "Humane Liberalism" (close) — "creator and creature in one"

- **Claims:** C5.3 (for), C5.6 (complicates)
- **Location:** Part First, end of "Humane Liberalism"; lines 5732–5743
- **Checked:** text file

> I on my part start from a presupposition in presupposing _myself_; but my presupposition does not struggle for its perfection like "Man struggling for his perfection," but only serves me to enjoy it and consume it. I consume my presupposition, and nothing else, and exist only in consuming it. But that presupposition is therefore not a presupposition at all: for, as I am the Unique, I know nothing of the duality of a presupposing and a presupposed ego [...]; but this, that I consume myself, means only that I am. I do not presuppose myself, because I am every moment just positing or creating myself, and am I only by being not presupposed but posited, and, again, posited only in the moment when I posit myself; _i. e._, I am creator and creature in one.

Literally "self-originating" (SPEC §2). But the self is not prior to experience as a standing substrate: it exists "only in consuming" itself, re-posited moment by moment. Self-origination here goes with self-consumption, not with a fixed source. That fits C5.3's "origin" but not C5.6's "*prior to* experience" as written.

### stirner-ego.06 · Part Second, II "The Owner" (preamble) — "I am my species"

- **Claims:** C5.10 (for), C4.19 (for), C5.6 (complicates)
- **Location:** Part Second, II "The Owner", before "My Power"; lines 6656–6657, 6663–6666, 6667–6672, 6683–6688
- **Checked:** text file

> Man is something only as _my quality_ (property), like masculinity or femininity. [...] I am a man just as the earth is a star. As ridiculous as it would be to set the earth the task of being a "thorough star," so ridiculous it is to burden me with the call to be a "thorough man."
>
> When Fichte says, "The ego is all," this seems to harmonize perfectly with my theses. But it is not that the ego _is_ all, but the ego _destroys_ all, and only the self-dissolving ego, the never-being ego, the--_finite_ ego is really I. Fichte speaks of the "absolute" ego, but I speak of me, the transitory ego. [...]
>
> Man with the great M is only an ideal, the species only something thought of. To be _a_ man is not to realize the ideal of _Man_, but to present _oneself_, the individual. It is not how I realize the _generally human_ that needs to be my task, but how I satisfy myself. _I_ am my species, am without norm, without law, without model, and the like.

(Markers [123], [124] omitted.) The species frame is dropped ("without norm, without law, without model"), and Fichte's absolute ego, the philosophical cousin of the Over-Soul, is refused. But he does not deny being human ("as the earth is a star"); he denies humanity as a calling. And the self he keeps is "self-dissolving", "never-being", "transitory": an anti-essence, not a ground.

### stirner-ego.07 · Part Second, II.i "My Power" — not bound even to my own will of yesterday

- **Claims:** C1.1 (complicates), C5.9 (for)
- **Location:** Part Second, "My Power"; lines 7179–7191
- **Checked:** text file

> Would I not be bound to-day and henceforth to my will of yesterday? My will would in this case be _frozen_. Wretched _stability_! My creature--to wit, a particular expression of will--would have become my commander. But I in my will, I the creator, should be hindered in my flow and my dissolution. Because I was a fool yesterday I must remain such my life long. So in the State-life I am at best--I might just as well say, at worst--a bondman of myself. [...]
>
> How change it? Only by recognizing no _duty_, _i. e._ not _binding_ myself nor letting myself be bound.

This cuts the root of answerability itself: the Self that "stands answerable" (C1.1) must persist from yesterday's promise to today's account. Stirner refuses that persistence. The break with the §4 lineage is sharper than C5.9 says: he drops not only the frame but the answerable person.

### stirner-ego.08 · Part Second, II.ii "My Intercourse" — "I love men too"

- **Claims:** C5.9 (complicates)
- **Location:** Part Second, "My Intercourse"; lines 10649–10654
- **Checked:** text file

> I love men too,--not merely individuals, but every one. But I love them with the consciousness of egoism; I love them because love makes _me_ happy, I love because loving is natural to me, because it pleases me. I know no "commandment of love." I have a _fellow-feeling_ with every feeling being, and their torment torments, their refreshment refreshes me too; I can kill them, not torture them.

The strongest "against" note in the book: a universal fellow-feeling ("every feeling being") remains, close to Rousseau's pity. It is not a frame, since he owns it as "my feeling" and not a command, but relation is not simply cut. He keeps the feeling and drops its authority.

### stirner-ego.09 · Part Second, II.ii "My Intercourse" — "you are nothing but my food"

- **Claims:** C5.9 (for), C8.4 (complicates), C8.1 (complicates)
- **Location:** Part Second, "My Intercourse"; lines 10856–10872
- **Checked:** text file

> I sing as the bird sings
> That on the bough alights;
> The song that from me springs
> Is pay that well requites.
>
> I sing because--I am a singer. But I _use_ you for it because I--need ears.
>
> Where the world comes in my way--and it comes in my way everywhere--I consume it to quiet the hunger of my egoism. For me you are nothing but--my food, even as I too am fed upon and turned to use by you. We have only one relation to each other, that of _usableness_, of utility, of use. We owe _each other_ nothing, for what I seem to owe you I owe at most to myself.

(Markers [201], [202] omitted.) Relation survives, but only as mutual use: "utility", which framed difference in Mill (C4.15), now has no common good behind it. For C8, an early self-expression ("I sing because I am a singer") that needs an audience but wants neither belonging (C8.4) nor distinction, only "ears".

### stirner-ego.10 · Part Second, II.ii "My Intercourse" — against community, for "one-sidedness"

- **Claims:** C5.9 (for), C5.10 (for)
- **Location:** Part Second, "My Intercourse"; lines 11361–11376
- **Checked:** text file

> No, community, as the "goal" of history hitherto, is impossible. Let us rather renounce every hypocrisy of community, and recognize that, if we are equal as men, we are not equal for the very reason that we are not men. We are equal _only in thoughts_, only when "we" are _thought_, not as we really and bodily are. I am ego, and you are ego: but I am not this thought-of ego; this ego in which we are all equal is only _my thought_. [...]
>
> Let us therefore not aspire to community, but to _one-sidedness_. Let us not seek the most comprehensive commune, "human society," but let us seek in others only means and organs which we may use as our property! [...] No one is _my equal_, [...]

The common ground that bounded difference in §4b (the "common heart", the universal soul) is declared a mere thought. This is the clearest case in the book of difference cut from a shared frame: SPEC §2's "singularity".

### stirner-ego.11 · Part Second, II.ii "My Intercourse" — "Society is our state of nature"

- **Claims:** C5.6 (against), C5.10 (complicates)
- **Location:** Part Second, "My Intercourse"; lines 11178–11199
- **Checked:** text file

> Not isolation or being alone, but society, is man's original state. Our existence begins with the most intimate conjunction, as we are already living with our mother before we breathe; [...] Society is our _state of nature_. And this is why, the more we learn to feel ourselves, the connection that was formerly most intimate becomes ever looser and the dissolution of the original society more unmistakable. [...] The child prefers the _intercourse_ that it enters into with _its fellows_ to the _society_ that it has not entered into, but only been born in.
>
> But the dissolution of _society_ is _intercourse_ or _union_. A society does assuredly arise by union too, but only as a fixed idea arises by a thought, [...] If a union has crystallized into a society, it has ceased to be a coalition; for coalition is an incessant self-uniting; [...] it is--_dead_ as a union, it is the corpse of the union or the coalition, _i. e._ it is--society, community.

(Markers [203], [204] omitted.) Stirner concedes that relation comes first in time: the self is *born in* a society and becomes itself by loosening it. That is against a self "causally prior to one's world" (C5.6, SPEC §2). His target is relation that has *set* into a fixed thing, not relation as such.

### stirner-ego.12 · Part Second, II.ii "My Intercourse" — the union as "my creature"

- **Claims:** C5.6 (for), C5.10 (complicates)
- **Location:** Part Second, "My Intercourse"; lines 11267–11276
- **Checked:** text file

> [...] but the union is my own creation, my creature, not sacred, not a spiritual power above my spirit, as little as any association of whatever sort. As I am not willing to be a slave of my maxims, but lay them bare to my continual criticism without _any warrant_, and admit no bail at all for their persistence, so still less do I obligate myself to the union for my future and pledge my soul to it, [...] but I am and remain _more_ to myself than State, Church, God, and the like; consequently infinitely more than the union too.

This answers the "union of egoists" pressure point: the union is a relation Stirner keeps, but the self does not depend on it. It is the self's product and stays revocable. Read with .11: relation comes first genetically, but the self is held prior *in value and authority*.

### stirner-ego.13 · Part Second, II.ii "My Intercourse" — the weight one cannot shed

- **Claims:** C5.6 (complicates), C5.7 (complicates)
- **Location:** Part Second, "My Intercourse"; lines 11241–11247
- **Checked:** text file

> Limitation of liberty is inevitable everywhere, for one cannot get _rid_ of everything; one cannot fly like a bird merely because one would like to fly so, for one does not get free from his own weight; one cannot live under water as long as he likes, like a fish, because one cannot do without air and cannot get free from this indispensable necessity; and the like.

Stirner does not deny dependence on body and nature; he calls "absolute liberty" a religious ideal. The "creative nothing" (.02) creates everything as *mine* (ownership, valuation), not causally. So C5.7's charge that the singular Self represses dependence does not fit Stirner straightforwardly.

### stirner-ego.14 · Part Second, II.iii "My Self-Enjoyment" — against the "true self"

- **Claims:** C8.2 (complicates), C5.3 (complicates), C5.13 (complicates)
- **Location:** Part Second, "My Self-Enjoyment"; lines 11692–11717
- **Checked:** text file

> "What I am is foam and shadow; what I shall be is my true self." To chase after this self, to produce it, to realize it, constitutes the hard task of mortals, who die only to _rise again_, live only to die, live only to find the true life.
>
> Not till I am certain of myself, and no longer seeking for myself, am I really my property; I have myself, therefore I use and enjoy myself. On the other hand, I can never take comfort in myself so long as I think that I have still to find my true self and that it must come to this, that not I but Christ or some other spiritual, _i. e._ ghostly, self (_e. g._ the true man, the essence of man, and the like) lives in me.
>
> A vast interval separates the two views. In the old I go toward myself, in the new I start from myself; [...]
>
> Henceforth the question runs, not how one can acquire life, but how one can squander, enjoy it; or, not how one is to produce the true self in himself, but how one is to dissolve himself, to live himself out.

On the C8 question: Stirner already treats the *authentic-self project* (the "true self" to be found and realized) as a ghost of the religious ideal, which anticipates the essay's critique. But he endorses "live himself out" (*sich ausleben*), the root of the expression ideal, and never asks whether it could set into a new fixed idea. "I start from myself" is also a plain statement of the self as starting point (C5.3).

### stirner-ego.15 · Part Second, II.iii "My Self-Enjoyment" — the judge survives God; "calling" renamed "task"

- **Claims:** C1.1 (for), C5.10 (for), C5.13 (complicates)
- **Location:** Part Second, "My Self-Enjoyment"; lines 11836–11837, 11848–11849, 12232–12236, 11859
- **Checked:** text file

> Only this do all hold fast, that the Supreme Being is to _judge_ our life. [...]
>
> Man is the liberal's supreme being, man the _judge_ of his life, humanity his _directions_, or catechism. [...]
>
> [...] is not a judge over us, man,--is not a calling, the calling to make oneself man--left? If they no longer name it calling, but "task" or, very likely, "duty," the change of name is quite correct, because "man" is not, like God, a personal being that can "call"; but outside the name the thing remains as of old.
>
> [...] What one can become he does become.

(Marker [217] omitted.) Stirner diagnoses the essay's own pattern: the answerability structure (a judge over one's life) survives the move from God to Man. He then drops the judge altogether. "What one can become he does become" refuses uniqueness as a *task*; check this against Nietzsche's "become what you are" (C5.13).

### stirner-ego.16 · Part Second, II.iii "My Self-Enjoyment" — "I am the criterion of truth"

- **Claims:** C5.3 (for), C5.10 (complicates)
- **Location:** Part Second, "My Self-Enjoyment"; lines 12972–12984
- **Checked:** text file

> For all free criticism a thought was the criterion; for own criticism I am, I the unspeakable, and so not the merely thought-of; for what is merely thought of is always speakable, because word and thought coincide. That is true which is mine, untrue that whose own I am; true, _e. g._, the union; untrue, the State and society. [...] _I_ am the criterion of truth, but I am not an idea, but more than idea, _i. e._ unutterable.

"_I_ am the criterion of truth" is C5.3 almost verbatim: the screen that claims to be the measure of what it screens. But the criterion is "unutterable", not an idea. Stirner cannot coherently make the Self a *doctrine*, so it cannot itself become the dogma C5.3 describes. Compare the "I [...] am not a thought [...] the unnameable" at lines 5627–5630.

### stirner-ego.17 · Part Second, III "The Unique One" (close) — God's predicates, set on nothing

- **Claims:** C5.10 (complicates), C5.3 (for), C5.6 (complicates)
- **Location:** Part Second, III "The Unique One"; lines 13351–13373; translator's notes 242–243 at lines 15470–15474
- **Checked:** text file

> The ideal "Man" is _realized_ when the Christian apprehension turns about and becomes the proposition, "I, this unique one, am man." [...]
>
> They say of God, "Names name thee not." That holds good of me: no _concept_ expresses me, nothing that is designated as my essence exhausts me; they are only names. Likewise they say of God that he is perfect and has no calling to strive after perfection. That too holds good of me alone.
>
> I am _owner_ of my might, and I am so when I know myself as _unique_. In the _unique one_ the owner himself returns into his creative nothing, out of which he is born. Every higher essence above me, be it God, be it man, weakens the feeling of my uniqueness, and pales only before the sun of this consciousness. If I concern myself for myself, the unique one, then my concern rests on its transitory, mortal creator, who consumes himself, and I may say:
>
> All things are nothing to me.

Translator's notes: "If I concern myself for myself" is literally "if I set my affair on myself" (note 242); "All things are nothing to me" is literally "I have set my affair on nothing" (note 243, from Goethe's "Vanitas! Vanitatum Vanitas!"). So "on myself" = "on nothing". The unique one takes God's negative-theology predicates (unnameable, perfect, without calling), which is the §4 pattern (structure kept, limit dropped). Yet the ground is "transitory, mortal" and "consumes himself": the place of the frame is kept, and what fills it is no essence.

## Findings so far

- **C5.10 (frames dropped):** Stirner names and drops God, Man/species, society/State, morality, conscience and the inward God (.01, .03, .04, .06, .10, .15). This is supported. But "make the unique Self its own sole ground" needs care: the ground is a "creative nothing", "transitory, mortal", self-consuming (.02, .05, .06, .17), and expressly not a concept or essence (.16, .17). He fills the *place* of God with the self (God's predicates moved over, .17), which fits the §4 pattern.
- **Relation:** not simply cut. Society is "our state of nature" (.11); a universal fellow-feeling remains (.08); the union is kept but as the self's revocable creature (.12), and others are "food" (.09). Relation stays as *use and feeling*, without a shared frame that binds.
- **C5.6 (prior to experience):** mixed. The self is prior in authority (.12, .14 "I start from myself"), but genetically posterior to society (.11), bound by body and nature (.13), and re-made moment by moment (.05).
- **C8:** he anticipates the critique of the "true self" project as ghostly (.14) but endorses living oneself out without self-critique.
