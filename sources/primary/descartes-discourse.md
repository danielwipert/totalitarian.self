### descartes-discourse.01 · Part IV — the cogito, and the mind needing no place

- **Claims:** C4.6 (for), C5.8 (for)
- **Location:** Part IV; `texts/descartes-discourse.txt` lines 880–900
- **Checked:** text file

> [...] this truth, I think, therefore I am (COGITO ERGO SUM), was so certain and of such evidence that no ground of doubt, however extravagant, could be alleged by the sceptics capable of shaking it [...] I thence concluded that I was a substance whose whole essence or nature consists only in thinking, and which, that it may exist, has need of no place, nor is dependent on any material thing; so that "I," that is to say, the mind by which I am what I am, is wholly distinct from the body, and is even more easily known than the latter, and is such, that although the latter were not, it would still continue to be all that it is.

This is the strongest text yet for the Self as *prior to the world* (C5.8): the "I" needs no place, depends on nothing material, and would continue to exist without the body. Note that this is the 1637 popular statement; compare *Meditations* VI (descartes-meditations.02), where Descartes qualifies it.
