# Nietzsche on uniqueness and self-becoming

- **Edition used:** "Schopenhauer as Educator" (Collins); *Joyful Wisdom* (Cohn); *Zarathustra* (Common); *Beyond Good and Evil* (Zimmern)
- **Text file:** `texts/nietzsche-thoughts-out-of-season-2.txt`, `texts/nietzsche-gay-science.txt`, `texts/nietzsche-zarathustra.txt`, `texts/nietzsche-beyond-good-and-evil.txt` (all clean)

- **Citation form:** work + section number, plus file and line. Abbreviations: SE ("Schopenhauer as Educator"), GS (*Gay Science* = *Joyful Wisdom*), Z (*Zarathustra*), BGE (*Beyond Good and Evil*).
- **Section numbers:** GS and BGE numbers in these translations match the standard numbering for every section cited here. SE sections are the essay's own roman numerals. Z is cited by Part and chapter title (Common's chapter numbers in brackets).
- **Claims to test:** C5.9, C5.13, C5.3, C5.6, C8.*

## Reading summary

Nietzsche does two things at once, and they do not reconcile.

1. **He drops the old frames by name.** God is dead and "we" killed him (GS §125); the "one norm, 'the man'" of monotheism was mankind's "greatest danger", and the individual may now set up "his *own* ideal" without apology to a God (GS §143); there is no "common good" (BGE §43); the higher man is "judge for thyself, and avenger of thy law" (Z I, "The Way of the Creating One") and takes his tests "before ourselves and before no other judge" (BGE §41). "Become what you are" is verified (GS §270, §335; Z IV, "The Honey Sacrifice"), and in GS §335 it is glossed "the new, the unique, the incomparable, making laws for ourselves and creating ourselves". This is the closest any §5b text so far comes to C5.9 as written.

2. **But he dismantles exactly the Self that SPEC §2 calls singular.** The "I" is a grammatical fiction (BGE §§16–17, 54); the will is a "social structure composed of many souls" whose ruling part falsely says "L'EFFET C'EST MOI" (BGE §19); *causa sui*, "to pull oneself up into existence by the hair", is "the best self-contradiction that has yet been conceived", and Nietzsche names it as the wish "to absolve God, the world, ancestors, chance, and society" (BGE §21). Consciousness, the faculty by which one would know oneself as unique, is herd property and returns only one's "averageness" (GS §354). The Self that remains is the body (Z I, "Despisers of the Body").

3. **And he puts a new frame where the old ones were.** It is not the Self. It is (a) *rank*: the higher individual is bred by an aristocratic society and grows on it "supported by it" like a climbing plant on an oak (BGE §§257–258); (b) *the type and its cultivation*: the individual life has value "only by your living for the good of the rarest and most valuable types" (SE VI), and becoming oneself is something a "trainer, a training-master" does to others (Z IV); (c) *the earth and the Superman*: "remain true to the earth", man is a bridge (Z Prologue §3); (d) *nature and necessity*: self-creation requires being "physicists", "discoverers of all the laws and necessities in the world" (GS §335), and the eternal recurrence asks the self to will its whole world, "this spider" included (GS §341).

So: the frame that is dropped is God, the common, the herd and equality. The frame that is kept or added is rank, type, body, earth and necessity. Uniqueness is affirmed as a fact of *action and body* (GS §335, §354) and as a *task* for a few (SE I, Z I), but denied as a property of the conscious I and denied as self-origination. Nietzsche is the first text in the series that cuts difference from the *common* frame; he is also the sharpest critic of the Self-as-source (C5.3). On §8: yes, he sees the herd speaking the language of freedom and self-valuation (BGE §44 "wrongly named 'free spirits'", §261 vanity as democratised self-esteem, §262 the morality of mediocrity that cannot "avow what it is").

**Caution on dates.** SE (1874) still has Schopenhauerian metaphysics: Nature "needs" the philosopher, artist and saint, and the saint is one "in whom the ego has melted away" (SE V). The frame there is metaphysical Nature, closer to Emerson than to BGE. The God-free, rank-framed position is the later one (GS 1882/1887, Z 1883–85, BGE 1886).

## Passages

### nietzsche-sing.01 · SE I — each man "a unique being"; "Be thyself!"; "answer for our existence to ourselves"

- **Claims:** C5.13 (for: uniqueness as task), C5.9 (complicates), C4.15 (complicates), C8.4 (complicates)
- **Location:** "Schopenhauer as Educator" §I; `texts/nietzsche-thoughts-out-of-season-2.txt` lines 2636–2644, 2659–2662, 2695–2702
- **Checked:** text file

> At bottom every man knows well enough that he is a unique being, only once on this earth; and by no extraordinary chance will such a marvellously picturesque piece of diversity in unity as he is, ever be put together a second time. He knows this, but hides it like an evil conscience;--and why? From fear of his neighbour, who looks for the latest conventionalities in him, and is wrapped up in them himself. But what is it that forces the man to fear his neighbour, to think and act with his herd, and not seek his own joy? [...] The man who will not belong to the general mass, has only to stop "taking himself easily"; to follow his conscience, which cries out to him, "Be thyself! all that thou doest and thinkest and desirest, is not--thyself!"
> [...]
> the wonderful fact of our existing at this present moment of time gives us the greatest encouragement to live after our own rule and measure; [...] We have to answer for our existence to ourselves; and will therefore be our own true pilots, and not admit that our being resembles a blind fortuity.

Uniqueness ("only once on this earth") stated as fact and made a duty, with the herd as its enemy; and answerability turned reflexive ("answer [...] to ourselves"). That is C5.9's "answerable to nothing beyond itself" nearly word for word. But the voice that says "Be thyself" is still *conscience*, and the uniqueness is "a work of nature" (line 2656), so the answerability structure is kept and its court moved inward, as in Rousseau (C4.17).

### nietzsche-sing.02 · SE I — "thy true being lies not deeply hidden in thee, but an infinite height above thee"

- **Claims:** C5.6 (against), C5.3 (against), C5.13 (complicates)
- **Location:** SE §I; lines 2736–2748
- **Checked:** text file

> Set up these things that thou hast honoured before thee, and, maybe, they will show thee, in their being and their order, a law which is the fundamental law of thine own self. Compare these objects, consider how one completes and broadens and transcends and explains another, how they form a ladder on which thou hast all the time been climbing to thy self: for thy true being lies not deeply hidden in thee, but an infinite height above thee, or at least above that which thou dost commonly take to be thyself. The true educators and moulders reveal to thee the real groundwork and import of thy being, [...] thy educators can be nothing but thy deliverers.

The self is found by what one has loved and by one's educators, and it lies *above* the self, not inside it as a source. It is not prior to experience: it is read off one's attachments. Against singularity as SPEC §2 defines it.

### nietzsche-sing.03 · SE V — "a vast community, held together [...] by [...] culture"; the saint, "in him the ego has melted away"

- **Claims:** C5.9 (against, for this text), C4.15 (for: the frame kept), C4.19 (complicates: an Emersonian structure)
- **Location:** SE §V; lines 3896–3903, 3915–3918
- **Checked:** text file

> In the first place, the new duties are certainly not those of a hermit; they imply rather a vast community, held together not by external forms but by a fundamental idea, namely that of _culture_; though only so far as it can put a single task before each of us--to bring the philosopher, the artist and the saint, within and without us, to the light, and to strive thereby for the completion of Nature. [...]
>
> Finally, Nature needs the saint. In him the ego has melted away, and the suffering of his life is, practically, no longer felt as individual, but as the spring of the deepest sympathy and intimacy with all living creatures:

In 1874 the self-becoming of SE I sits inside a community of culture and a metaphysical Nature that "needs" its highest types. The frame is not dropped; it is changed from God to Nature, much as Emerson's "common nature" (C4.19). The highest type here is the one in whom the ego *melts*.

### nietzsche-sing.04 · SE VI — "Mankind must toil unceasingly to bring forth individual great men"; the individual life's value is "living for the good of the rarest"

- **Claims:** C5.13 (for: rank kept), C5.9 (against), C5.6 (against)
- **Location:** SE §VI; lines 3951–3953, 3974–3984
- **Checked:** text file

> "Mankind must toil unceasingly to bring forth individual great men: this and nothing else is its task." [...] For the problem is--"In what way may your life, the individual life, retain the highest value and the deepest significance? and how may it least be squandered?" Only by your living for the good of the rarest and most valuable types, not for that of the majority,--who are the most worthless types, taken as individuals. This way of thinking should be implanted and fostered in every young man's mind: he should regard himself both as a failure of Nature's handiwork and a testimony to her larger ideas. "She has succeeded badly," he should say; "but I will do honour to her great idea by being a means to its better success."

The key text for the key question. The ordinary individual is told his value lies *outside* him, in serving the higher type, and that he is "a means". This is rank as frame, the opposite of the Self as its own ground. Uniqueness is valued, but it is the uniqueness of the few, and everyone else's meaning is derived from them.

### nietzsche-sing.05 · GS §117 — "condemned 'to be an individual'": individual independence is recent

- **Claims:** C4.13 (complicates), C4.15 (complicates), C4.10 (complicates), C5.9 (complicates)
- **Location:** *Joyful Wisdom* §117 "The Herd's Sting of Conscience"; `texts/nietzsche-gay-science.txt` lines 4299–4312
- **Checked:** text file

> In the longest and remotest ages of the human race there was quite a different sting of conscience from that of the present day. At present one only feels responsible for what one intends and for what one does, and we have our pride in ourselves. All our professors of jurisprudence start with this sentiment of individual independence and pleasure, as if the source of right had taken its rise here from the beginning. But throughout the longest period in the life of mankind there was nothing more terrible to a person than to feel himself independent. To be alone, to feel independent, neither to obey nor to rule, to represent an individual—that was no pleasure to a person then, but a punishment; he was condemned "to be an individual."

Nietzsche makes the essay's own historicising move: individual responsibility (Locke's forensic person, C4.10) is late, and the jurists wrongly treat it "as if the source of right" had begun there. Useful for C4.13: the herd-conscience came before the individual one. But he does not treat the late form as a mutation to regret; it is the condition of his project.

### nietzsche-sing.06 · GS §143 — polytheism as the "preliminary to the justification of the selfishness and sovereignty of the individual"

- **Claims:** C5.9 (for), C5.13 (for: God dropped as frame), C4.15 (for: Nietzsche sees difference as once framed by gods)
- **Location:** *Joyful Wisdom* §143 "The Greatest Utility of Polytheism"; lines 4777–4783, 4791–4800
- **Checked:** text file

> For the individual to set up his _own_ ideal and derive from it his laws, his pleasures and his rights—_that_ has perhaps been hitherto regarded as the most monstrous of all human aberrations, and as idolatry in itself; in fact, the few who have ventured to do this have always needed to apologise to themselves, usually in this wise: "Not I! not I! but _a God_, through my instrumentality!" [...] It was here that individuals were first permitted, it was here that the right of individuals was first respected. The inventing of Gods, heroes and supermen of all kinds, [...] was the inestimable preliminary to the justification of the selfishness and sovereignty of the individual: the freedom which was granted to one God in respect to other Gods, was at last given to the individual himself in respect to laws, customs and neighbours.

The strongest "for" text. Nietzsche describes the very pattern of §4/§4b (difference allowed only under a divine frame, "Not I! but a God") and approves its removal: sovereignty passes "at last" to "the individual himself". Compare Emerson's "God is here within" (emerson-selfreliance.01): Nietzsche names the God-alibi and drops it.

### nietzsche-sing.07 · GS §125 — the madman: "Shall we not ourselves have to become Gods, merely to seem worthy of it?"

- **Claims:** C5.13 (for: God dropped), C5.3 (complicates), C5.9 (complicates)
- **Location:** *Joyful Wisdom* §125 "The Madman"; lines 4479–4484, 4496–4497
- **Checked:** text file

> "Where is God gone?" he called out. "I mean to tell you! _We have killed him_,—you and I! We are all his murderers! But how have we done it? How were we able to drink up the sea? Who gave us the sponge to wipe away the whole horizon? What did we do when we loosened this earth from its sun? Whither does it now move? Whither do we move? Away from all suns? [...] Is not the magnitude of this deed too great for us? Shall we not ourselves have to become Gods, merely to seem worthy of it?

The frame's loss is stated as a loss ("wipe away the whole horizon", no "above and below"), not a liberation. The self-as-God is posed as a question and a burden, not a claim. Evidence that Nietzsche *sees* the vacancy C5.3 describes; it does not show that he fills it with the Self.

### nietzsche-sing.08 · GS §270 — "Thou shalt become what thou art."

- **Claims:** C5.13 (for: the phrase verified)
- **Location:** *Joyful Wisdom* §270; lines 5837–5839
- **Checked:** text file

> _What Saith thy Conscience?_—"Thou shalt become what thou art."

Verifies C5.13's "to verify" phrase in this translation. Note its form: it is the voice of *conscience* and a "shalt", an imperative, so the answerability structure (the self addressed and commanded) survives inside the formula.

### nietzsche-sing.09 · GS §335 — "the new, the unique, the incomparable, making laws for ourselves and creating ourselves!"; but "we must be _physicists_"

- **Claims:** C5.9 (for), C5.6 (for: difference absolute; complicates: self not prior to world), C5.13 (for), C5.3 (complicates)
- **Location:** *Joyful Wisdom* §335 "Cheers for Physics!"; lines 7186–7196, 7215–7221
- **Checked:** text file

> For it is selfishness in a person to regard _his_ judgment as universal law, and a blind, paltry and modest selfishness besides, because it betrays that you have not yet discovered yourself, that you have not yet created for yourself any individual, quite individual ideal:—for this could never be the ideal of another, to say nothing of all, of every one!——He who still thinks that "each would have to act in this manner in this case," has not yet advanced half a dozen paces in self-knowledge: otherwise he would know that there neither are nor can be similar actions,—that every action that has been done, has been done in an entirely unique and inimitable manner [...] We, however, _would seek to become what we are_,—the new, the unique, the incomparable, making laws for ourselves and creating ourselves! And for this purpose we must become the best students and discoverers of all the laws and necessities in the world. We must be _physicists_ in order to be _creators_ in that sense,

The nearest any source so far comes to SPEC §2's "unique, self-originating": the ideal "could never be the ideal of another", there "neither are nor can be similar actions" (difference made absolute, against Kant's universal law). But the next sentence binds self-creation to "all the laws and necessities in the world": the self creates *within* necessity, not prior to it. It is self-legislating, but not causally prior to its world.

### nietzsche-sing.10 · GS §290 — "give style" to one's character: self-fashioning as constraint upon a given nature

- **Claims:** C5.13 (complicates), C5.6 (complicates), C5.3 (complicates)
- **Location:** *Joyful Wisdom* §290 "One Thing is Needful"; lines 6171–6173, 6182–6187, 6200–6203
- **Checked:** text file

> To "give style" to one's character—that is a grand and a rare art! He who surveys all that his nature presents in its strength and in its weakness, and then fashions it into an ingenious plan, [...] In the end, when the work has been completed, it is revealed how it was the constraint of the same taste that organised and fashioned it in whole or in part: whether the taste was good or bad is of less importance than one thinks,—it is sufficient that it was _a taste_!—It will be the strong imperious natures which experience their most refined joy in such constraint, [...] For one thing is needful: namely, that man should _attain to_ satisfaction with himself—be it but through this or that fable and artifice:

Self-creation works on "all that his nature presents", a given material ("first nature"), under "constraint". Two edges: it is not origination from nothing; but the standard is internal and contentless ("sufficient that it was _a taste_"), and self-satisfaction may rest on "fable and artifice". The latter is a seed of the screen that knows it is a screen (C5.2), not one that forgets it.

### nietzsche-sing.11 · GS §354 — consciousness is herd property: self-knowledge yields "averageness"

- **Claims:** C5.3 (for: Nietzsche as ally), C8.2 (for), C8.5 (complicates), C5.6 (complicates)
- **Location:** *Joyful Wisdom* §354 "The 'Genius of the Species'"; lines 8058–8072
- **Checked:** text file

> my idea is that consciousness does not properly belong to the individual existence of man, but rather to the social and gregarious nature in him; that, as follows therefrom, it is only in relation to communal and gregarious utility that it is finely developed; and that consequently each of us, in spite of the best intention of _understanding_ himself as individually as possible, and of "knowing himself," will always just call into consciousness the non-individual in him, namely, his "averageness";—that our thought itself is continuously as it were _outvoted_ by the character of consciousness—by the imperious "genius of the species" therein—and is translated back into the perspective of the herd. Fundamentally our actions are in an incomparable manner altogether personal, unique and absolutely individual—there is no doubt about it; but as soon as we translate them into consciousness, they _do not appear so any longer_....

The most important text for §8. Uniqueness is real ("absolutely individual"), but only below consciousness. The *conscious* attempt to know oneself individually is "outvoted" by the herd and returns "averageness". So the Self that speaks of its uniqueness speaks a herd language: very close to C8.2 (conformity in the vocabulary of self-expression), and it gives C8.5 a deeper root (no individual vocabulary *can* exist, because vocabulary is communal). It also cuts against the Cartesian/Lockean Self (consciousness as identity, C4.6, C4.9).

### nietzsche-sing.12 · GS §341 — the eternal recurrence: the self must will its whole world, "this spider" included

- **Claims:** C5.6 (against), C5.9 (complicates)
- **Location:** *Joyful Wisdom* §341 "The Heaviest Burden"; lines 7408–7413, 7419–7426
- **Checked:** text file

> "This life, as thou livest it at present, and hast lived it, thou must live it once more, and also innumerable times; and there will be nothing new in it, but every pain and every joy and every thought and every sigh, and all the unspeakably small and great in thy life must come to thee again, and all in the same series and sequence—and similarly this spider and this moonlight among the trees, [...] If that thought acquired power over thee, as thou art, it would transform thee, and perhaps crush thee; [...] Or, how wouldst thou have to become favourably inclined to thyself and to life, so as _to long for nothing more ardently_ than for this last eternal sanctioning and sealing?—

The test of the highest self is not self-origination but affirmation of a given sequence it did not make, with "nothing new in it". The self is *in* its world (spider, moonlight) and is asked to will that world whole: "to thyself *and* to life". Against C5.6's "prior to experience".

### nietzsche-sing.13 · Z Prologue §3, §5 — "REMAIN TRUE TO THE EARTH"; the last man: "Every one wanteth the same; every one is equal"

- **Claims:** C5.13 (for: God dropped; complicates: a new frame), C5.9 (against), C8.3 (complicates)
- **Location:** *Zarathustra*, Prologue §3 and §5; `texts/nietzsche-zarathustra.txt` lines 848–851, 858–859, 1063–1064
- **Checked:** text file

> The Superman is the meaning of the earth. Let your will say: The Superman SHALL BE the meaning of the earth!
>
> I conjure you, my brethren, REMAIN TRUE TO THE EARTH, and believe not those who speak unto you of superearthly hopes! [...]
>
> Once blasphemy against God was the greatest blasphemy; but God died, and therewith also those blasphemers. To blaspheme the earth is now the dreadfulest sin,
>
> [§5] No shepherd, and one herd! Every one wanteth the same; every one is equal: he who hath other sentiments goeth voluntarily into the madhouse.

God goes, but "sin" stays and is moved to the earth: a new frame, and the meaning lies in the Superman, *beyond* the present self. The last man is the herd without a shepherd: sameness, not individuality-talk. So the prologue's §8 picture is of uniformity, not of conformity *disguised* as self-expression (for that see .11, .23, .25).

### nietzsche-sing.14 · Z I, "The Despisers of the Body" [IV] — "Body am I entirely"; the Self "is thy body"; the ego its instrument

- **Claims:** C4.1 (for: body as limit kept), C5.8 (against the Self-as-Mind), C5.3 (for: ally), C5.6 (complicates)
- **Location:** *Zarathustra* I, "The Despisers of the Body"; lines 1700–1711, 1721–1730, 1752–1754, 1760–1761
- **Checked:** text file

> “Body am I, and soul”—so saith the child. And why should one not speak like children?
>
> But the awakened one, the knowing one, saith: “Body am I entirely, and nothing more; and soul is only the name of something in the body.”
>
> The body is a big sagacity, a plurality with one sense, a war and a peace, a flock and a shepherd.
>
> An instrument of thy body is also thy little sagacity, my brother, which thou callest “spirit”—a little instrument and plaything of thy big sagacity. [...]
>
> Instruments and playthings are sense and spirit: behind them there is still the Self. [...] Behind thy thoughts and feelings, my brother, there is a mighty lord, an unknown sage—it is called Self; it dwelleth in thy body, it is thy body. [...]
>
> The creating Self created for itself esteeming and despising, it created for itself joy and woe. The creating body created for itself spirit, as a hand to its will. [...]
>
> No longer can your Self do that which it desireth most:—create beyond itself.

The body is not kept as a limit beside the Self; it *becomes* the Self, and the ego/spirit becomes its tool. The direct reverse of the Cartesian Self = Mind (C5.8), and a strong ally passage. But note the complication: this body-Self is "the creating Self" that "created for itself esteeming and despising", so it is still a source of values, and its aim is to "create beyond itself". It is a source, but a plural, unconscious, bodily one, not the I.

### nietzsche-sing.15 · Z I, "The Thousand and One Goals" [XV] — "the individual himself is still the latest creation"; "As yet humanity hath not a goal"

- **Claims:** C5.13 (complicates), C5.9 (against), C4.15 (for), C5.3 (complicates)
- **Location:** *Zarathustra* I, "The Thousand and One Goals"; lines 2830–2852, 2869–2874
- **Checked:** text file

> Values did man only assign to things in order to maintain himself—he created only the significance of things, a human significance! Therefore, calleth he himself “man,” that is, the valuator. [...]
>
> Creating ones were first of all peoples, and only in late times individuals; verily, the individual himself is still the latest creation. [...]
>
> Older is the pleasure in the herd than the pleasure in the ego: and as long as the good conscience is for the herd, the bad conscience only saith: ego. [...]
>
> A thousand goals have there been hitherto, for a thousand peoples have there been. Only the fetter for the thousand necks is still lacking; there is lacking the one goal. As yet humanity hath not a goal.

Value-creation is first collective; the individual is a late *creation* (made, not original). And Zarathustra does not end in a thousand individual goals but asks for "the one goal" and a "fetter" for humanity. That is a new shared frame he wants, not its absence.

### nietzsche-sing.16 · Z I, "The Way of the Creating One" [XVII] — "A first motion? A self-rolling wheel?"; "judge for thyself, and avenger of thy law"

- **Claims:** C5.9 (for), C5.6 (for, as ideal; complicates, as a test most fail), C5.13 (for), C5.3 (complicates)
- **Location:** *Zarathustra* I, "The Way of the Creating One"; lines 2961–2969, 2977–2978, 2987–2998, 3060–3061, 3074
- **Checked:** text file

> Wouldst thou go into isolation, my brother? Wouldst thou seek the way unto thyself? Tarry yet a little and hearken unto me.
>
> “He who seeketh may easily get lost himself. All isolation is wrong”: so say the herd. And long didst thou belong to the herd.
>
> The voice of the herd will still echo in thee. [...]
>
> Art thou a new strength and a new authority? A first motion? A self-rolling wheel? Canst thou also compel stars to revolve around thee? [...]
>
> Free, dost thou call thyself? Thy ruling thought would I hear of, and not that thou hast escaped from a yoke. [...]
>
> Free from what? What doth that matter to Zarathustra! Clearly, however, shall thine eye show unto me: free FOR WHAT?
>
> Canst thou give unto thyself thy bad and thy good, and set up thy will as a law over thee? Canst thou be judge for thyself, and avenger of thy law? [...]
>
> Thou lonesome one, thou goest the way of the creating one: a God wilt thou create for thyself out of thy seven devils! [...]
>
> I love him who seeketh to create beyond himself, and thus succumbeth.—

Here is SPEC §2's singularity in so many words: "a first motion", "a self-rolling wheel", the self as its own lawgiver, judge and avenger, answerable to no court but itself. Strongest "for" passage. But it is put as a *test* most fail ("show me thine authority"), mere escape from the yoke is despised, and the end is to "create beyond himself, and thus succumb", not to rest in the Self. Also "a God wilt thou create": the frame-slot is refilled, not abolished.

### nietzsche-sing.17 · Z IV, "The Honey Sacrifice" [LXI] — "Become what thou art!" spoken by "a trainer, a training-master"

- **Claims:** C5.13 (for: phrase verified; complicates: its context is pedagogy and rank)
- **Location:** *Zarathustra* IV, "The Honey Sacrifice"; lines 10272–10278
- **Checked:** text file

> Until, biting at my sharp hidden hooks, they have to come up unto MY height, the motleyest abyss-groundlings, to the wickedest of all fishers of men.
>
> For THIS am I from the heart and from the beginning—drawing, hither-drawing, upward-drawing, upbringing; a drawer, a trainer, a training-master, who not in vain counselled himself once on a time: “Become what thou art!”

The formula comes from someone who draws others "UP to me": self-becoming is set inside a relation of height and training (*Züchter*, breeder). It is relational and hierarchical, not self-enclosed.

### nietzsche-sing.18 · BGE §§17, 54 — "a thought comes when 'it' wishes, and not when 'I' wish"; "I" as "a synthesis which has been MADE by thinking"

- **Claims:** C5.3 (for: ally against the Self-as-source), C5.8 (for), C4.6 / C4.7 (complicates), C5.13 (pressure point verified)
- **Location:** *Beyond Good and Evil* §17, §54; `texts/nietzsche-beyond-good-and-evil.txt` lines 598–603, 1686–1690
- **Checked:** text file

> [§17] a thought comes when "it" wishes, and not when "I" wish; so that it is a PERVERSION of the facts of the case to say that the subject "I" is the condition of the predicate "think." ONE thinks; but that this "one" is precisely the famous old "ego," is, to put it mildly, only a supposition, an assertion, and assuredly not an "immediate certainty."
>
> [§54] The attempt was then made, with marvelous tenacity and subtlety, to see if one could not get out of this net,--to see if the opposite was not perhaps true: "think" the condition, and "I" the conditioned; "I," therefore, only a synthesis which has been MADE by thinking itself.

Confirms the C5.13 pressure point: the "I" is conditioned, not the condition. That is the reverse of C5.3's Self that "claims to be the origin of what it sees". §16 (lines 560–597) makes the same attack on "I think" as an "immediate certainty", aimed at Descartes.

### nietzsche-sing.19 · BGE §19 — the will is "a social structure composed of many souls"; "L'EFFET C'EST MOI"

- **Claims:** C5.3 (for: Nietzsche diagnoses the I's claim to be the source), C5.6 (for, as diagnosis), C5.8 (for)
- **Location:** BGE §19; lines 657–660, 680–686
- **Checked:** text file

> [...] Inasmuch as in the given circumstances we are at the same time the commanding AND the obeying parties, [...] In this way the person exercising volition adds the feelings of delight of his successful executive instruments, the useful "underwills" or under-souls--indeed, our body is but a social structure composed of many souls--to his feelings of delight as commander. L'EFFET C'EST MOI. what happens here is what happens in every well-constructed and happy commonwealth, namely, that the governing class identifies itself with the successes of the commonwealth.

This is C5.3 as a psychology: the I takes credit as cause ("the effect is me") for what a plurality did. Nietzsche calls it an illusion. The model he puts in its place is political and plural (commonwealth, governing class), so the Self is a relation of rank *inside* the body (compare .14).

### nietzsche-sing.20 · BGE §21 — *causa sui*: "to absolve God, the world, ancestors, chance, and society" and "pull oneself up into existence by the hair"

- **Claims:** C5.6 (for: names the shift; against: Nietzsche rejects it), C5.9 (against, for Nietzsche), C5.3 (for: ally), C7.1 (complicates; cross-check with nietzsche-guilt)
- **Location:** BGE §21; lines 725–735
- **Checked:** text file

> The CAUSA SUI is the best self-contradiction that has yet been conceived, it is a sort of logical violation and unnaturalness; but the extravagant pride of man has managed to entangle itself profoundly and frightfully with this very folly. The desire for "freedom of will" in the superlative, metaphysical sense, such as still holds sway, unfortunately, in the minds of the half-educated, the desire to bear the entire and ultimate responsibility for one's actions oneself, and to absolve God, the world, ancestors, chance, and society therefrom, involves nothing less than to be precisely this CAUSA SUI, and, with more than Munchausen daring, to pull oneself up into existence by the hair, out of the slough of nothingness.

The single most useful passage for the essay. Nietzsche gives SPEC §2's "self-originating, causally prior to one's world" a name (*causa sui*), lists the frames it cuts (God, world, ancestors, chance, society), ties it to total self-responsibility (C7.1), and calls it a self-contradiction held by "the half-educated". By his own lights he is not the singular Self; he is its first diagnostician. But he replaces free/unfree will with "STRONG and WEAK wills" (line 755): rank again.

### nietzsche-sing.21 · BGE §41 — tests "before ourselves and before no other judge"; "Not to cleave to any person [...] Not to cleave to one's own liberation"

- **Claims:** C5.9 (for: answerable to no other judge; difference cut from relation), C5.3 (complicates)
- **Location:** BGE §41; lines 1331–1338, 1342–1345, 1350–1351
- **Checked:** text file

> One must subject oneself to one's own tests that one is destined for independence and command, and do so at the right time. One must not avoid one's tests, although they constitute perhaps the most dangerous game one can play, and are in the end tests made only before ourselves and before no other judge. Not to cleave to any person, be it even the dearest--every person is a prison and also a recess. Not to cleave to a fatherland, [...] Not to cleave to one's own liberation, to the voluptuous distance and remoteness of the bird, [...] Not to cleave to our own virtues, [...] One must know how TO CONSERVE ONESELF--the best test of independence.

Difference cut from relation, almost literally: every person "a prison", no fatherland, no judge but oneself. For C5.9. But the detachment extends to one's own "liberation" and "virtues": the free one does not cleave to a Self-ideal either. It describes a discipline for those "destined for [...] command", not a general human claim.

### nietzsche-sing.22 · BGE §44 — "wrongly named 'free spirits'": the herd in the vocabulary of freedom

- **Claims:** C8.2 (for), C8.3 (complicates), C8.4 (complicates), C6.* (note: "and the same in America")
- **Location:** BGE §44; lines 1386–1396
- **Checked:** text file

> In every country of Europe, and the same in America, there is at present something which makes an abuse of this name a very narrow, prepossessed, enchained class of spirits, who desire almost the opposite of what our intentions and instincts prompt--[...] Briefly and regrettably, they belong to the LEVELLERS, these wrongly named "free spirits"--as glib-tongued and scribe-fingered slaves of the democratic taste and its "modern ideas" all of them men without solitude, without personal solitude,

Direct §8 evidence: the vocabulary of freedom ("free spirit") taken over by "men without solitude" who want "the universal, green-meadow happiness of the herd" (lines 1401–1402). This is the herd speaking the language of independence. It is not yet *self-expression* (C8.2's term); the content Nietzsche objects to is equality and comfort. America named.

### nietzsche-sing.23 · BGE §§257–258 — "EVERY elevation of the type 'man'" needs "a long scale of gradations of rank"; the higher grows "supported by" society

- **Claims:** C5.13 (for: rank kept), C5.9 (against), C5.6 (against), C4.15 (for)
- **Location:** BGE §257, §258; lines 5055–5059, 5096–5108
- **Checked:** text file

> [§257] EVERY elevation of the type "man," has hitherto been the work of an aristocratic society and so it will always be--a society believing in a long scale of gradations of rank and differences of worth among human beings, and requiring slavery in some form or other. Without the PATHOS OF DISTANCE, such as grows out of the incarnated difference of classes, [...]
>
> [§258] that it should therefore accept with a good conscience the sacrifice of a legion of individuals, who, FOR ITS SAKE, must be suppressed and reduced to imperfect men, to slaves and instruments. Its fundamental belief must be precisely that society is NOT allowed to exist for its own sake, but only as a foundation and scaffolding, by means of which a select class of beings may be able to elevate themselves to their higher duties, and in general to a higher EXISTENCE: like those sun-seeking climbing plants in Java--they are called Sipo Matador,--which encircle an oak so long and so often with their arms, until at last, high above it, but supported by it, they can unfold their tops in the open light, and exhibit their happiness.

The answer to the key question in Nietzsche's own image. The higher individual is *not* self-originating: a society produces it ("the work of an aristocratic society") and it stands "supported by" that society like a vine on an oak. Its difference is relational in the strictest sense, a "pathos of distance" that needs those below. The frame is rank, and it is harsh (slavery, "instruments"), but it is a frame.

### nietzsche-sing.24 · BGE §260 — the noble type "does not require to be approved of"; "he is a CREATOR OF VALUES"

- **Claims:** C5.9 (for), C5.3 (complicates), C5.13 (for: rank)
- **Location:** BGE §260; lines 5177–5186
- **Checked:** text file

> The noble type of man regards HIMSELF as a determiner of values; he does not require to be approved of; he passes the judgment: "What is injurious to me is injurious in itself;" he knows that it is he himself only who confers honour on things; he is a CREATOR OF VALUES. He honours whatever he recognizes in himself: such morality equals self-glorification. In the foreground there is the feeling of plenitude, of power, which seeks to overflow, [...]

The self as its own ground of value ("What is injurious to me is injurious in itself") fits C5.9 and C5.3's Self-as-origin. But the subject is a *type* and a *caste* ("a ruling caste, pleasantly conscious of being different from the ruled", lines 5156–5157), not the individual as such, and its difference is defined against those it ranks. §265 adds that the noble soul "honours HIMSELF" in his equals and treats "the exchange of honours and rights" as "the ESSENCE of all intercourse" (lines 5474–5481): relation among peers is kept.

### nietzsche-sing.25 · BGE §261 — vanity: the democratised wish to "think well" of oneself, ruled by the "older propensity" to wait for others' opinion

- **Claims:** C8.2 (for), C8.4 (for: an internal engine, the wish for others' good opinion), C7.10 (complicates)
- **Location:** BGE §261; lines 5284–5290, 5299–5308
- **Checked:** text file

> from time immemorial, in all social strata in any way dependent, the ordinary man WAS only that which he PASSED FOR:--not being at all accustomed to fix values, he did not assign even to himself any other value than that which his master assigned to him (it is the peculiar RIGHT OF MASTERS to create values). It may be looked upon as the result of an extraordinary atavism, that the ordinary man, even at present, is still always WAITING for an opinion about himself, and then instinctively submitting himself to it; [...] In fact, conformably to the slow rise of the democratic social order [...] the originally noble and rare impulse of the masters to assign a value to themselves and to "think well" of themselves, will now be more and more encouraged and extended; but it has at all times an older, ampler, and more radically ingrained propensity opposed to it--and in the phenomenon of "vanity" this older propensity overmasters the younger. The vain person rejoices over EVERY good opinion which he hears about himself (quite apart from the point of view of its usefulness, and equally regardless of its truth or falsehood), just as he suffers from every bad opinion: for he subjects himself to both,

The closest Nietzsche comes to C8's mechanism. Self-valuation is democratised, but in most people it turns into its opposite: dependence on others' opinion, sought and then obeyed. That is self-esteem's vocabulary carrying an engine of subjection, much as C8.2 has it. Compare Rousseau's "opinion of others" (C4.18).

### nietzsche-sing.26 · BGE §262 — when the old discipline breaks, "the individual dares to be individual and detach himself"; then "be like them! become mediocre!"

- **Claims:** C5.9 (for, as historical description; complicates, as Nietzsche's verdict), C8.2 (complicates), C1.2 (complicates)
- **Location:** BGE §262; lines 5351–5358, 5368–5373, 5386–5394
- **Checked:** text file

> With one stroke the bond and constraint of the old discipline severs: it is no longer regarded as necessary, as a condition of existence--[...] the individual dares to be individual and detach himself. [...] The dangerous and disquieting point has been reached when the greater, more manifold, more comprehensive life IS LIVED BEYOND the old morality; the "individual" stands out, and is obliged to have recourse to his own law-giving, his own arts and artifices for self-preservation, self-elevation, and self-deliverance. Nothing but new "Whys," nothing but new "Hows," no common formulas any longer, [...] except one species of man, the incurably MEDIOCRE. The mediocre alone have a prospect of continuing and propagating themselves--they will be the men of the future, the sole survivors; "be like them! become mediocre!" is now the only morality which has still a significance, which still obtains a hearing.--But it is difficult to preach this morality of mediocrity! it can never avow what it is and what it desires! it has to talk of moderation and dignity and duty and brotherly love--it will have difficulty IN CONCEALING ITS IRONY!

Nietzsche's own account of frame loss: a phase of luxury in which "no common formulas" remain and each individual must be his own lawgiver, ending in "decay" and the rule of the mediocre. He narrates C5.9 as a historical event and does not simply praise it. The mediocre conceal themselves in borrowed moral vocabulary ("brotherly love"), not in individuality-talk, so this supports the *form* of C8.2 (a morality that "can never avow what it is") but not its specific vocabulary.

## Verdict by claim (proposed)

- **C5.13** (uniqueness as a task, God dropped, rank kept): **supported, with one rewording.** "Become what you are" is verified (.08, .09, .17); God is dropped as frame (.06, .07, .13); rank is kept and is load-bearing (.04, .23, .24). But "drops God as its frame" is too weak: Nietzsche puts new frames in God's place (earth, Superman, type, nature/necessity). See the suggested rewording in the report.
- **C5.9** (after Emerson, difference cut from frame; Self its own ground, answerable to nothing beyond itself): **challenged for Nietzsche.** For: .01, .06, .09, .16, .21, .24. Against: .02, .04, .12, .15, .20, .23. He cuts difference from the *common* frame (herd, equality, God) but not from relation (rank, breeding, body, earth), and he rejects the self-originating Self by name (.20).
- **C5.3**: Nietzsche is a strong **ally**: .14, .18, .19, .20, .11.
- **C5.6**: Nietzsche **names** the shift to self-origination (.20, .19) and rejects it; his own self is in its world (.09, .12). Absolute difference survives only at the level of action/body (.09, .11).
- **C8.2 / C8.4**: **for**, in part: .11 (self-knowledge returns herd averageness), .22 (herd in the vocabulary of freedom), .25 (democratised self-esteem as vanity). Nietzsche does not describe self-*expression* as the herd's vocabulary; he describes freedom, equality and self-esteem.
